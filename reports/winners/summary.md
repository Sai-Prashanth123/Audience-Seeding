## Article: the core format
- **Why X rewards it:** articles earn the most saves (8-10 per 1,000 views). People who save a post come back to
  it, and X counts long reading time. Saves were also the top signal in every client's biggest posts.
- **Title:** promise the complete system or guide, and put a number in it (titles with a number did about 1.4 times
  better). Shapes that won:
  - "How to Build Services-as-Software Business" (Alex, 867,071 views)
  - "The GTM Flywheel Behind $7M ARR (And Why We Killed Our Outbound Engine to Build It)" (Michel, 363,743)
  - "How to run paid ads from Claude Code [mega guide]" (Michel, 287,820)
  - "The 5-Step Outbound System I'd Build From Scratch After Running Outbound for 300+ Companies" (Alex, 222,552)
  - "How to Become a GTM Engineer: The Complete Guide" (Kenny, 97,742)
- Post it as the day's 1st post. Then push it with a quote post (below), not a second article.

## Quote post (QRT): the article's amplifier
- About 8 in 10 of their quote posts quote their **own** article or post. That is the right use.
- **Attach a video or image.** Alex: ~4,700 views with a video vs ~3,000 without. Michel: ~3,600 vs ~2,500.
  Peers show the same.
- **Why X rewards it:** a quote post puts the article in front of people again with a new reason to open it, and
  quotes and reposts are how a post reaches people outside the follower base. A video keeps people on the post longer.
- Short, punchy caption over the video: "this should not be free" (Michel, 556,673 views, video);
  "my co-founder after dropping the entire sauce for free" (Alex, 331,801, video).
- Quoting a teammate's post (Kenny, Ivan, the company) works best for Michel (~9,300 typical). Quoting strangers
  is the weakest choice.

## Video on its own: only with a giveaway or a real result
- As a standalone post it is average for Alex and weak for Michel. It works when the post is a **giveaway or a
  concrete result** and the video walks through it:
  - "We turned 24 employees into LinkedIn influencers in 90 days. 581 posts, 43,473 reactions..." (Michel, 66,962)
  - "We spent months building a Claude Code system that runs outbound... (and we're giving the whole thing away)"
    (Michel, 54,995)
- Otherwise attach the video to a quote post of the article instead.

## Thread: only the "then vs now" shape (and Kenny)
- For Alex and Michel, most threads land below a normal post now. The ones that still work are a **before/after
  contrast list**: "GTM back in the day (2023): ... GTM today: ..." (Kenny, 14,697, his best thread);
  "Distribution in 2023:" (Alex, his best thread of the period), or a story with money in it:
  "We offered $5,000 to whichever employee got the most engagement on LinkedIn..." (Michel, 440,240).
- Kenny: threads are working (5 of 6 big wins). Keep testing them.

## Plain tweet: giveaways only
- Plain tweets are the weakest format, except giveaways of a real asset with a money proof:
  - "I'm giving away the Claude Code skills we use to manage $300k/mo in ad spend at ColdIQ." (Michel, 445,347)
  - "We built 12 Claude Code skills that run our entire paid media ops across Google, Meta, and LinkedIn at ColdIQ
    (and we're giving the whole pack away)." (Alex, 207,748)
- Shape: what you're giving away, the money proof, who built it and how long it took, "comment X to get it".

## Posting rules that come from X's ranking code
- **Max 2 posts a day.** X shows the same author to the same follower less often with each extra post the same
  day. In their data the 3rd post got about half the views of the 2nd (Alex ~2,900 vs ~5,200; Michel ~1,800 vs ~3,700).
- Weekends are slightly better for Alex and for the peers.
- Best time window per client: audience-seeding app, Best times tab (refreshed daily).

## How this playbook was built (as of 25 Nov 2026)

**Data**
- Alex Vacca, Michel Lieben, Kenny Damian: every original X post from 1 Mar 2026 to 25 Nov 2026 that was at
  least 2 days old: 1,002 posts (Alex 450, Michel 461, Kenny 91).
- 12 similar accounts for comparison (3,356 posts): RetentionAdam, coldemailchris, clientascension, danielfazio,
  LoganTGott, cbwritescopy, nikolak47, VukConfidential, Vick_Mucunski, itsivanfalco, mailankhong, codyschneiderxx.
  They are used only to confirm patterns, never for voice.
- Earlier months were left out: the stored history before March 2026 only kept ~10 high-view posts a month and
  had a gap from Nov 2025 to Feb 2026, which made old formats (threads) look better than they were.
- Video vs image: X's public embed data for 3,104 posts.

**What counts as a win**
- Alex and Michel: 10,000+ views (Naga's rule).
- Kenny: 3 times his normal views at the time, or his own top 20% (his account is newer).
- Every post is also compared with that person's normal views at the time it went out, so a big month for the
  whole account doesn't count as a winning format.

**How rules were found** (three separate checks had to agree)
1. A model that estimates each feature's effect on views for each person, sharing strength across the three
   (and the peers) so Kenny's fewer posts don't give wild answers.
2. A second, different model (gradient-boosted trees) to catch combinations, e.g. quote post + video.
3. 570 pairs of posts by the same person on the same topic within a month where one did twice as well:
   what was different between the winner and the loser.

**How rules were tested**
- Learned only from posts before the last 90 days, then checked on the last 90 days (360 posts it had never seen).
- Posts that followed the rules most vs least: Alex ~7,400 vs ~2,500 views; Michel ~4,300 vs ~1,850;
  Kenny ~7,700 vs ~2,100. Chance this is luck: under 1 in 500 for each.
- Given a strong and a weak draft, the rules pick the stronger one about 2 times out of 3.
- One rule was dropped because the checks disagreed (more line breaks).
